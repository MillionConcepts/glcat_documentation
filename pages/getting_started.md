# Getting Started

The Galaxy Evolution Explorer (GALEX) was a NASA Small Explorer (SMex) class astronomical space telescope that observed the sky in two ultraviolet bands between April 2003 and June 2013. GALEX used a large field of view (~1.25°) to build up extensive survey coverage (~30,000 square degrees) in UV wavelenghts that are difficult to observe from the ground and are poorly surveyed by any other mission or instrument. The two bands were called "Near Ultraviolet" (NUV, ca. 2271Å) and "Far Ultraviolet (FUV, ca. 1528Å). The micro-channel plate detectors (MCP) recorded the position and time of individual photons hitting the detector, typically with a time resolution of 5 thousadnths of a second. The spacecraft could observe for up to ~30 minutes of every ~90 minute orbit, while in Earth's shadow. The GALEX data therefore have time domain coverage spanning from ~seconds to ~years. The GALEX data are unique and unprecedented in a number of ways, and no missions that can match its capabilities in total sky coverage, photometric depth, or time domain capabilities are currently in development.

For some further high-level explication, [Wikipedia has a good overview of the mission design and objectives.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GALEX)
